# Othello – Take-Home

## Act III, Scene 3 (lines 30–105)
Iago never makes a single direct accusation in this stretch of the scene. Instead, he uses three techniques: he echoes Othello's own words back as a question, he denies thinking something he's just implied, and he interrupts himself mid-thought. Track how this works and explain why it's effective.

Read lines 30–105 carefully (Cassio's exit through Othello's "Was not that Cassio parted from my wife?" and what follows). Take notes and mark specific lines to support your answers.

1. Find at least three moments where Iago echoes, denies, or interrupts instead of stating something directly. Quote each one and identify which of the three techniques it is.

2. For each moment you quoted, explain what Othello is left to infer in the silence. What does Othello hear that Iago never actually says?

3. Iago tells Othello to "beware of jealousy" rather than accusing Desdemona of anything. Why does this indirect approach work better on Othello than a direct accusation would have? Consider what we already know about Othello's character and self-regard from earlier in the play.

4. By the end of this stretch (around line 105), Othello asks Iago to "speak to me as to thy thinkings." What has changed in the relationship between the two men in just these 75 lines? Use specific moments from the text to support your answer.
