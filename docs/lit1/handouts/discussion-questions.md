# Othello Discussion Questions

## Act 1

1. What are Iago and Roderigo arguing about before they wake Brabantio (1.1.39–64)? What reliable information does this exchange provide about their characters—and about Cassio
and Othello?

2. What kind of language do Iago and Roderigo use to describe Othello and Desdemona’s marriage (1.1.76–134)? How does this language shape our understanding of their attitudes?

3. What effect does this language have on Brabantio (1.1.137–171)? 

4. How does Brabantio respond to Roderigo’s courtship of Desdemona before and after learning of her marriage to Othello (1.1.92–95; 1.1.172)? What does this shift suggest about Othello’s position in Venetian society?

5. Identify discrepancies between Iago’s public behavior and his private intentions in this scene. How does the audience’s awareness of these contradictions affect our interpretation of later scenes in which Iago appears?

6. How does Othello’s behavior in Act I, scene ii differ from what Act I, scene i might have led
you to expect?

7. According to Brabantio, how has Othello persuaded Desdemona to marry him (1.2.61–78)? Does Brabantio suggest this explanation earlier (1.1.168–171)?

8. What are the Duke and Senators discussing at the beginning of Act I, scene iii (1.3.1–43)? Why might Shakespeare devote so many lines to their debate about the Turkish fleet’s
destination?

9. Do the Duke’s and First Senator’s responses to Brabantio’s accusations resemble their earlier discussion of the Turkish threat (1.3.106–114)? In what ways?

10. In what sense does Othello admit guilt regarding Desdemona—both in “stealing” her and in
using “witchcraft” (1.3.78–81; 1.3.90–94; 1.3.168)?

11. In 1.3.158, the Folio reads “a world of kisses,” while the Quarto reads “a world of sighs.” Which version seems more consistent with the dramatic situation and with Desdemona’s
character? Why?

12. What reasons do Othello and Desdemona give for her accompanying him to Cyprus (1.3.243–269)? Which of the two demonstrates greater self-knowledge?

13. What advice does Iago most persistently give Roderigo (1.3.335–367)? Why does he repeat
it so insistently?

## Act 2

1. In what order do the characters arrive in Cyprus (2.1)? What dramatic purposes are served by
this sequence of arrivals?

2. Comment on Othello’s lines to Desdemona, “If it were now to die / ’Twere now to be most
happy” (2.1.187-188).

3. Both Cassio and Iago suggest that Othello is deeply devoted to Desdemona—“our great captain’s captain” (2.1.74; cf. 2.3.314–318). What evidence in Othello’s behavior upon landing
in Cyprus supports this view (2.1.179–210)?

4. Iago claims to suspect both Othello and Cassio of adultery with Emilia (2.1.291–307). To what extent do these suspicions logically follow from the cynical view of women he expresses in his exchange with Desdemona (2.1.127–158)?

5. Comment on Othello’s lines: “The purchase made, the fruits are to ensue: / That profit’s yet to
come ’tween me and you” (2.3.9-10)

6. What is Iago trying to do when he tells Cassio Desdemona is “sport for Jove” (2.3.19)? Where is the humor in this short exchange? What does Iago mean by “happiness to their sheets”
(2.3.30)?

7. Are there any echoes between the drunken brawl in Act 2 and the opening scene outside Brabantio’s house in Act 1? List the similarities.

8. What is ironic about Iago’s claim, “I do love Cassio well and would do much / To cure him of this evil” (2.3.140–141)?

9. As Othello investigates the brawl during the night watch, he remarks that his “best judgment” is nearly overcome (2.3.203–206). What causes this near loss of self-control?

10. What is Cassio saying when he talks about “reputation” (2.3.280)?

11. “So will I turn her virtue into pitch, / And out of her own goodness make the net / That shall
enmesh them all” (2.3.360–362). What specific plan is Iago describing? Why does this strategy give him particular satisfaction?

12. What does Iago mean by “Thou know’st we work by wit and not by witchcraft, / And wit depends on dilatory time” (2.3.392-394)?
